What it actually does

Not features. Just what happens under the hood.

No claims we can't stand behind — this is the plain description of the mechanism.

Cross-board coverage

LinkedIn, Indeed, Naukri, and Wellfound — watched at whatever pace each one actually tolerates, not one fixed schedule for all four.

Duplicate collapsing

The same recruiter's post on three boards, under three slightly different titles, becomes one entry in your queue — not three.

Paced, not bulk-pulled

No burst requests, no request pattern that reads as a script instead of a person checking back now and then.

Fails loud, not silent

If a source changes its markup or goes dark overnight, you're told — not left staring at a queue that quietly stopped updating.

Not a pitch — this is the feed

Four sources. One queue. Zero duplicates.

The actual pattern Anchor runs: new postings arrive from each board, get compared against what you've already seen, and either land in your queue or get quietly dropped.

The pipeline

What happens between "posted" and "in your inbox"

Three real steps, in order — this part isn't decoration.

01 01 — LISTEN

Watch each source on its own clock

Every board has a different rhythm and a different tolerance for being watched. Anchor checks each one at a pace that looks like a person, not a script.

02 02 — CLEAN

Collapse the same job, wherever it's posted

Recruiters post the same role on three boards under three slightly different titles. Anchor matches them and keeps one.

03 03 — DELIVER

Land in a queue you actually check

New, deduped, and ranked by how close it is to what you told Anchor you're looking for. Nothing older than what you've already dismissed.

Why this exists
A note from the two of us, not a testimonial

We built Anchor because we were both job hunting at the same time and kept describing the same problem to each other: not too few listings, too many copies of the same nine listings. We're not going to invent a user count to make this feel bigger than it is. Right now it's the two of us, a handful of friends testing it, and a waitlist. If that changes, we'll tell you honestly — and if it doesn't, we'd still rather you knew.
